# CS 101: Introduction to Programming

CS 101 is the entry point of this curriculum. Students with no prior experience
learn to read, write, and debug small programs in Python, a high-level
interpreted language widely used as a first teaching language[^python-wiki].

## What it covers

The course works through variables, control flow, functions, and basic data
handling, using the official Python tutorial as its lab companion[^python-tutorial].
[Recursion](/topics/recursion.md) is introduced near the end of the term, once
students are comfortable with function calls, and [Big-O Notation](/topics/big-o.md)
is previewed so students arrive in [CS 201](/courses/cs201.md) able to talk
about efficiency.

## Why it comes first

[CS 201](/courses/cs201.md) lists this course as its prerequisite: you cannot
analyze data structures without first being fluent in a language. The course
opens with a lecture on [Grace Hopper](/instructors/grace-hopper.md), the naval
officer who built one of the first compilers[^hopper-wiki], to show students why
programming languages exist at all.

## Reading

The recommended companion is [Structure and Interpretation of Computer
Programs](/resources/sicp.md), whose early chapters reinforce exactly this
material and are freely available online[^sicp].
